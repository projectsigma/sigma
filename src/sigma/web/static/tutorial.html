Overview

Project SIGMA · method walk-through

From establishments to a spatial–economic influence network

SIGMA takes three inputs: mapped business locations, a road network, and a national input–output table. sigma load turns the locations into typed establishments and builds sectors for the study area (stages 1–5). sigma run measures road distances, finds clusters of each sector, rolls up sectors that have no cluster, refines dispersed clusters once with the network Gap statistic, and then computes centers, territories, graph X, Katz centrality and establishment scores (stages 6–17). Explorer then draws road routes on request. Each stage below has a worked, interactive example. The examples are illustrative; the panel “Current SIGMA run” shows the published results of the area you opened.

Two units of analysis

An establishment pOne mapped place with a location and one economic type, for example one bakery.
An X node n = (t, c)A cluster c of establishments of the same type t, for example a local group of bakeries.

Centrality is computed for X nodes. Each clustered establishment receives its cluster's centrality, reduced by at most 15% for its road distance from the cluster center. An establishment labeled noise belongs to no cluster, so it is not part of any X node.

A sector (economic type t) is not fixed in advance. Stage 5 builds the sector list of each study area from the national 240-industry table, and stage 9 can merge a sector that has no cluster into a broader sector.

What the model does not say

SIGMA builds an explicit model of potential spatial–economic structure. It does not observe buyer–seller contracts.

A modeled edge is not evidence that a particular shop buys from a particular factory. A centrality score is not measured output, causal impact, or welfare.

Part 1 · Stages 1–5 · sigma load

From observed places to typed establishments and area sectors

Two map sources describe the same streets. SIGMA decides which records are the same place, classifies each place's activity, attaches input–output (IO) industries with their evidence, and then chooses the sectors that this study area can support.

1Stage 1

Two map sources, one study area

SIGMA reads places from OpenStreetMap (OSM), taken from the Geofabrik Philippines extract, and from Overture Maps Places. Both layers are clipped to the study boundary. Names, categories, IDs, license data, and source provenance are kept.

  • No direct Foursquare data. Overture records attributed to Foursquare are excluded.
  • The same Geofabrik file also supplies the roads used later.

The same bakery can appear once in OSM and once in Overture. SIGMA must decide which rows describe the same place before it counts places.

2Stage 2 · candidate pairs

Same place or not? Distance and name decide

SIGMA compares one OSM record with one Overture record. It measures their distance d with the Haversine formula, the great-circle distance on a sphere:

d = 2R arcsin √h,   h = sin²(Δφ/2) + cos φ1 cos φ2 sin²(Δℓ/2)

φ = latitude, ℓ = longitude (radians), R = 6,371,008.8 m

The name score s is the larger of two RapidFuzz scores divided by 100: the simple ratio, and the token-sort ratio, which ignores word order (“Rosa Bakery” and “Bakery Rosa” give s = 1). The farther apart two records are, the more similar the names must be.

30 m 0.90

Accepted pairs get a ranking strength q = 0.82 s + 0.18 (1 − d/120).

2Stage 2 · matching

Rank the pairs, then match one-to-one

Accepted pairs are sorted by q (highest first), then by distance, then by stable source IDs. SIGMA accepts a pair only if neither record is already matched.

A close pair with a weaker name (q = 0.873) ranks above a far pair with an exact name (q = 0.850).

2Stage 2 · output

One canonical record per matched pair

A matched pair becomes one canonical place at the midpoint of its two points. If the midpoint falls outside the study boundary, an in-boundary source point is used. Unmatched records stay as single-source places.

Canonical IDs are derived with SHA-256 from the source IDs, so every row can be traced back to its sources.

The thresholds and the q weights are a versioned matching policy. They are not learned, and they are not a proven-optimal record-linkage method.

3Stage 3 · activity

Classify each place's activity (PSIC)

Name, OSM tags, and source categories go through reviewed deterministic crosswalks and name rules to a PSIC code (Philippine Standard Industrial Classification). PSIC describes the activity, for example baking or retail sale.

  • Strong source categories take precedence. A name rule that disagrees with a trusted category is dropped (NAME_RULE_OVERRULED_BY_CATEGORY).
  • Compatible OSM tag combinations are allowed. Incompatible combined activities are sent to review; they are not forced into one code.
  • No large language model (LLM) is used in the default path.

Stage 4 then translates the activity into IO industries, and stage 5 into the sectors of this area. The sector decides which places may cluster together (Part 2) and which economic coefficients apply to them (Part 3).

3Stage 3 · office or service

A government office is not a school or a hospital

Some places are offices that administer health or education, for example a schools division office. Their source category can still say “school” or “hospital”. After the PSIC classifier, SIGMA applies one narrow rule set to the name:

  • A clear administrative office name changes the activity to PSIC 84120 (public administration of health, education and other social programmes).
  • A name that also contains a frontline word (hospital, clinic, school, university and similar) is sent to review. Frontline services are never changed.
  • A name that only mentions an agency, such as “Department of Health”, is sent to review.

The previous PSIC is kept in admin_original_psic; the status is REASSIGNED, CONFIRMED (already 84xxx) or REVIEW. This rule decides the activity only. It never decides public or private ownership.

4Stage 4 · IO candidates

From one activity code to candidate IO industries

The PSA input–output (IO) tables describe the economy at three nested levels: 16, 80 and 240 industries. Published concordances map a PSIC code to a set of candidate industries at each level. A level receives a tag only when its set has exactly one member.

Example: PSIC class 0112 (growing of rice) is in group 011. Group 011 maps to 1 IO16 industry, 6 IO80 industries and 11 IO240 industries, so only the IO16 tag is known. A reviewed refinement row for class 0112 narrows IO240 to 001 Palay. Its parents then fix IO80 01 and IO16 01: the place is tagged at all three levels.

  • A refinement row may only narrow a set. A row that would add an industry is not applied.
  • Other evidence can choose inside a set but never outside it: same-name evidence, OSM tag rules for IO80, and ownership evidence (next step).
  • The three tags must nest. If they disagree, the weaker source's tag is cleared (LEVEL_CONFLICT) or replaced by the parent of a stronger finer tag (DERIVED_FROM_IO240, DERIVED_FROM_IO80).

io_mapping_provisional = true marks a mapping that depends on a draft bridge or refinement row, not on a published concordance.

4Stage 4 · ownership

Public or private? Only strong evidence changes the industry

Education and health are split into public and private IO industries: IO80 74 / 75 (IO240 229 / 230) and IO80 76 / 77 (IO240 231 / 232). A PSIC code alone often leaves both sides as candidates.

At places.classified, SIGMA can choose one side from evidence, in this order of strength: a verified link to a registry record, an official registry ID found in the source record, and an explicit ownership attribute in OSM or Overture. A registry match by name and city only, or a government operator tag, is recorded as provisional and does not change the industry. Evidence that points to both sides is a conflict, and SIGMA abstains.

Registry layers: packaged national school (BEIS) and higher-education (UniFAST) references, a validated national hospital snapshot when it is installed, and verified links in references/institution_links.json. Name heuristics used during tagging (for example “National High School”) are flagged ownership_inferred; they are not registry proof. The decision is in institution_promotion_status and institution_evidence_basis.

5Stage 5 · area sectors

Keep an industry only where enough places support it

For each study area, SIGMA chooses its own list of sectors from the national 240-industry table. An IO240 industry becomes a sector only when at least min_places places (5 by default) carry it as an exact tag. Other industries fold into a parent:

  • unkept siblings of a kept industry form the IO80 residual X:Y:o;
  • an IO80 industry with no kept child stays whole as X:Y when its places reach min_places;
  • the remaining industries of the IO16 parent form X:o, or the whole X when nothing was kept.
5

Each of the 240 industries belongs to exactly one sector, including industries with no observed place. Codes: X:Y:Z one IO240 industry; X:Y:o the rest of an IO80 parent; X:Y a whole IO80 parent; X:o the rest of an IO16 parent; X a whole IO16 parent.

5Stage 5 · coarse tags

Places with only an IO80 or IO16 tag

A coarse place joins a sector at once when one sector contains every industry under its tag. Otherwise a policy decides:

  • residual (default): the place goes to the residual of its parent (IO80_RESIDUAL, IO16_RESIDUAL). This is a recorded convention: the residual holds the least supported industries.
  • contain (optional): the place joins a sector only if that sector contains all of its candidate industries. A parent is kept whole when more than max_straddle_share of its places would straddle the split; remaining straddlers are unassigned.
0.25

The share applies only to contain. A place whose supplied candidate industries contradict its own tag is never imputed: it stays UNASSIGNED_CANDIDATE_CONFLICT under both policies.

5Stage 5 · the table

Aggregate the national table to the area's sectors

With membership matrix M (one row per sector, one column per IO240 industry, entries 0 or 1), SIGMA aggregates transactions and gross output:

Zarea = M Z MT,   xarea = M x

Rows and columns use the same partition, so total transactions and total output are unchanged. Part 3 then recomputes coefficients from the aggregated table.

The recomputed coefficient equals the output-weighted average of the merged coefficients, not their simple average.

+Between load and run · review

Review the handoff before analysis

sigma load ends by writing places/places.parquet. You may open it in a GIS or data tool, remove establishments that should not take part (for example non-MSME rows), and save it in place. Keep at least canonical_id, geometry, CRS and economy_code.

SIGMA hashes the file bytes. The next sigma run recomputes only the stages that depend on the changed file, and rebuilds the area sectors (stage 5) when they are older than the handoff.

Remove a row rather than add a flag: clustering uses every row it receives.

+Between load and run · augment

Add your own source with the same matching rule

sigma augment AREA FILE --source NAME reads a CSV or Parquet file with four required columns: source_id, name, lon, lat. Each record is compared with the loaded canonical places using the stage 2 rule (120 m, name score bands, one-to-one by q).

  • Matched: the canonical ID is kept, the source is added to provenance, and the position becomes the source-count weighted average. For a place with 2 sources: (2 · p + u) / 3.
  • Unmatched: a new place with its own deterministic ID, classified by the same routines.
  • Every other column is kept as aug_<source>__<column>. These columns do not enter the scores.

--check previews the result without writing. Applying the same file twice is recognized and refused.

+Between load and run · explicit tags

Explicit IO tags: derive parents, never children

A supplied record may carry its own io16, io80 and io240 codes. SIGMA derives the missing parents of the finest supplied code and checks every supplied parent link. It never guesses a finer code from a coarser one.

Supplied tags are national codes, never local residual codes such as X:Y:o; the next run places the tagged establishments into the area's sectors.

Part 2 · Stages 6–12 · sigma run

Road distances, clusters, and territories

Every spatial step in SIGMA measures distance along roads. This part builds the road graph, finds clusters of each sector, merges sectors that have no cluster into a broader sector, refines dispersed clusters once, picks a center for each final cluster, and assigns territory.

6Stage 6

Roads become a graph

GR = (VR, ER),   edge length ℓe > 0

Vertices are canonicalized road coordinates. Edges are the straight pieces between consecutive coordinates of each road line. Each edge has its projected length in metres (local UTM). The shortest road distance dR is computed with Dijkstra's algorithm (SciPy).

Here one road line with 4 coordinates gives 4 vertices and 3 edges: dR(v1, v4) = 120 + 90 + 150 = 360 m, while the straight line is about 312 m.

The graph is undirected: one-way tags are kept for audit but do not restrict routing.

6Stage 6 · topology

Where do two roads connect?

Roads come from the same Geofabrik file: the study boundary buffered by 5 km, motor-road classes only (motorway … residential, living_street, service, road), excluding access = no, private, agricultural, or forestry. Lines are projected to local UTM and not simplified.

Coordinates are rounded to 11 significant digits. Two lines connect only where they share a canonical vertex. A flyover crosses a road without a shared vertex, so no turn is possible there.

If a flyover were joined to the road below it, the graph would allow a turn that does not exist. That false path would change clusters, centers, territories, X edges, and scores.

7Stage 7

Snap each place onto the road

si = arg minz ∈ N ‖xi − z‖,    d(i, j) = dR(si, sj)

N is every position on every road, not only the vertices. Here xi is nearer the side street, so its snap point is on the side street, and d(i, j) = 115 + 170 = 285 m.

The snap distance (65 m and 55 m here) is stored for quality checks. It is not added to d(i, j).

If two snap points are on road parts that do not connect, their road distance is undefined (infinite). SIGMA does not replace it with a straight-line distance.

7Stage 7 · why roads

Why not straight-line distance?

Straight-line distance asks how close two coordinates are on a map: 330 m. Road distance asks how far apart they are through allowed roads: 690 m.

A river, disconnected roads, or a single crossing point can put two nearby places in different local groups.

What this metric does not represent: footpaths outside the motor-road profile, ferries, one-way rules, and travel time.

8Stage 8

Cluster each sector separately

Network HDBSCAN* finds dense, connected groups along roads without fixing the number of clusters in advance. It works on one economic type at a time, so here the Retail places are set aside while Food is clustered.

All establishments of the type take part at the same time. HDBSCAN* does not grow clusters from a chosen starting point; the next four steps show what it does instead.

Positions with no dense group are labeled noise. Noise is not used to build centers.

Defaults: min_cluster_size = 5; min_samples k defaults to the same value.

This is HDBSCAN* applied to road shortest-path distance. It is not ordinary latitude/longitude clustering.

8HDBSCAN* · step 1 of 4

No starting point: every position is p

HDBSCAN* does not pick a first point. It takes all positions of one economic type at once. A position is one snap point on the road. Establishments that snap to the same point are merged into one position with weight w (in this example every weight is 1).

For every position, SIGMA asks the same question: how far along the road must I go to reach k establishments, counting myself? The answer is that position's core distance. Each position takes the role of p once, and the order does not change the result.

core(p) = min { r ≥ 0 : Σq: d(p,q) ≤ r wq ≥ k }

Example with k = 3: position 190 m reaches itself, 220 m (30 m away), and 150 m (40 m away), so its core distance is 40 m. Position 0 m must go as far as 190 m, so its core distance is 190 m. A small core distance means crowded; a large one means sparse. Nothing is clustered yet.

3

SIGMA's default is k = 5; this example uses 3 so that it stays small, and the next three steps keep k = 3. This is different from k-means, which starts from chosen centers, and from the usual step-by-step description of DBSCAN, which picks an unvisited point and grows a cluster from it.

8HDBSCAN* · step 2 of 4

Which positions should be linked first?

Two positions should be linked early when they are close to each other and both are in crowded surroundings. The link cost, called mutual reachability, says exactly this:

dmr(p, q) = max{ core(p), core(q), d(p, q) }

Example: 150 m and 190 m are 40 m apart, but core(150) = 70 m, so their link costs 70 m. Position 150 is at the edge of its group, so it joins later than the positions in the middle.

SIGMA computes this cost for every pair within the search horizon. It then adds links from the cheapest to the most expensive and skips any link whose two ends are already connected. The 9 links that remain connect all 10 positions: the minimum spanning tree (a forest if some positions cannot reach each other).

Why the maximum? With plain road distance, a lone shop 40 m from a group would join as early as the group's own members. With core distances, sparse positions join late, so one sparse position cannot connect two dense groups early.

8HDBSCAN* · step 3 of 4

Lower the threshold and the tree breaks into groups

Keep only the links that cost at most ε. With a large ε, everything is one group. As ε gets smaller, the expensive links disappear and the tree breaks apart. A group with fewer than min_cluster_size establishments (3 here) is too small to be a cluster; at that level its positions are noise.

120 m

Read the tree from the top. Below 190 m, position 0 drops off. Below 170 m, position 620 drops off. Below 150 m, the rest splits into two groups of 4. Below 70, 60, and 50 m, those groups lose positions until they are too small.

HDBSCAN* writes the same thing with the density level λ = 1/ε: a smaller ε is a larger, denser λ.

8HDBSCAN* · step 4 of 4

Keep the clusters that persist

The condensed tree keeps only real splits, where both sides have at least min_cluster_size establishments. A candidate cluster C is born at λbirth(C), and each position p leaves it at λp. Stability adds up how long the positions stay:

S(C) = Σp ∈ C wp (λp − λbirth(C))

8HDBSCAN* · search horizon

Limit the road search

SIGMA never builds the full table of all pairs. It stores only pairs closer than the current radius, which grows over trials:

r0 = R / gn−1,   rt+1 = min(R, g rt)

With defaults R = 5,000 m, g = 1.5, n = 4: about 1,481, 2,222, 3,333, and 5,000 m.

  • Stop early when two successive trials give the same labels and memberships, and at most 1% of positions have a core distance cut by the radius.
  • Memory guard: at most 20 million stored pairs. If a larger trial exceeds it, the last successful trial is kept (flag pair_cap_limited).
  • If network EOM raises after neighbor-graph construction, SIGMA first retries network HDBSCAN leaf selection on the same distance graph. If network methods fail, the separately labeled Euclidean HDBSCAN recovery path runs by road component. A successful zero-cluster extraction is not itself a failure or a reason to invoke fallback.

5 km is a search horizon, not DBSCAN's ε. It limits which pairs are computed; it does not define clusters. Agreement between trials is a check, not a proof.

9Stage 9 · why K = 0

Why can a sector end with zero clusters?

By default HDBSCAN* is not allowed to return a whole sector as one cluster (allow_single_cluster = false). A sector keeps a cluster only when its density tree splits into at least two groups, and each group has at least min_cluster_size = 5 establishments.

So a sector with fewer than 2 × 5 = 10 establishments always has K = 0 retained clusters. Stage 5 can still keep such a sector, because it needs only 5 places. All its places are then noise: no X node and no score.

6 4

A successful extraction with K = 0 is a valid result. It is not an error, and it does not start the Euclidean recovery path.

9Stage 9 · residual-first trials

Try the nearest residual, keep the merge only if it retains more places

For a generated hierarchy, sigma run first clusters the stage 5 sectors without Gap refinement. For each sector with K = 0 it tries the nearest residual that already exists:

X:Y:Z → X:Y:o, then X:o  ·  X:Y and X:Y:o → X:o

The places of the failed sector and of the residual are clustered together with the same roads, snaps and HDBSCAN* settings. With b = retained places of these sectors before and a = retained places after the trial, the merge is accepted only if a > b.

Sectors that aim at the same residual are tried together. A rejected IO80 residual is followed by the IO16 residual in the next round. A sector with K ≥ 1 is never a source, whatever its noise share.

9Stage 9 · effects

A new effective sector list, with the original tag kept

  1. Each moved place keeps original_economy_code; its economy_code becomes the residual. The effective code governs clustering, the IO table, X and scores.
  2. The national table is aggregated again to the new partition (M Z MT, M x), and coefficients are recomputed.
  3. The result is written as a separate package under hierarchical/iterative_rollup/<hash>/ with rollup_audit.json.
  4. SIGMA clusters the effective sectors again without Gap refinement and stops if the cluster count differs from the trial prediction. Only then does stage 10 run.

The roll-up runs only for generated hierarchies. sigma run AREA --no-iterative-rollup keeps the stage 5 sectors. Fixed IO16, IO80, IO240, custom and --equivalent economies are not rolled up. Noise is never assigned to its nearest territory.

10Stage 10 · which clusters

One retained cluster can hold several local concentrations

HDBSCAN* decides which establishments belong to a cluster. It can keep a long, dispersed cluster as one. Stage 10 asks once, for each retained cluster of the final sectors: do several road medoids describe this cluster better than one?

  • Eligible: at least 2 × min_cluster_size = 10 members and at least 3 distinct road positions.
  • Noise stays noise. A sector with K = 0 is not rescued here; that was stage 9.
300 m

10Stage 10 · within-cluster cost

How far are members from k medoids?

For k = 1, 2, … SIGMA looks for k medoids among the members' own road positions. The cost is the total road distance from each member to its nearest medoid:

Wk = min Σi minj=1…k dR(qi, mj)

Greedy BUILD adds the medoid that lowers the cost most; SWAP then exchanges medoids while the cost strictly falls. This is PAM-style k-medoids, an approximation, not a proof of the optimum. Wk always falls as k grows, so its size alone cannot choose k.

2

10Stage 10 · reference draws

Compare with members placed at random on the same roads

The Gap statistic compares the observed cost with the cost of structureless data. SIGMA draws B = 10 reference samples. Each places the same number of members uniformly at random on the road vertices inside the cluster's bounding box, in the same road component, and repeats the k-medoid search.

Gap(k) = 1B Σb log W*k,b − log Wk

A large Gap means the members are much more concentrated around k medoids than random members on the same roads.

This road-vertex reference is SIGMA's adaptation. The published Gap statistic uses a uniform box in Euclidean space.

10Stage 10 · one-standard-error rule

Choose the smallest k that is good enough

first k with Gap(k) ≥ Gap(k+1) − sk+1
sk = sd(log W*k) · √(1 + 1/B)

This is the one-standard-error rule of Tibshirani, Walther and Hastie (2001). If no k up to the ceiling (6) satisfies it, the result is the ceiling with status upper_bound_reached, which flags the search limit, not an optimal k.

300 m

This page uses the browser's own random generator. SIGMA seeds NumPy with SHA-256 of (seed, sector, cluster), so every cluster has its own reproducible draws.

10Stage 10 · assignment and audit

Assign all members, check sizes, record the decision

  1. If k ≥ 2 is selected, all members are assigned to the nearest selected medoid by exact road distance.
  2. If any child has fewer than min_cluster_size members, or only one child remains, the original cluster is kept (no_admissible_partition).
  3. The child that holds the first member (by canonical_id) keeps the original cluster ID; other children get new IDs after the largest ID of the sector.
  4. Children are never tested again: one nonrecursive pass. Stage 11 computes a new full-network 1-median for each final child.

Clusters with more than 64 members fit the medoids on a reproducible sample of 64, then assign every member. The run uses up to 4 independent worker processes by default; each worker handles one cluster at a time, and the parent process assigns IDs in a fixed order, so 1 to 4 workers give the same result. --median-gap-workers 1 runs serially; --no-median-gap keeps unrefined HDBSCAN* clusters. The clustering artifact keeps hdbscan_cluster beside the final cluster and writes median_gap_audit.json.

11Stage 11

Which road node is easiest to reach from the whole cluster?

For a cluster with demand nodes q1, …, qm (one per establishment), score every road vertex v by total road distance:

M(v) = Σi=1…m dR(qi, v),   v* = arg minv ∈ VR M(v)
1,000 m

This is the classic 1-median (minisum) objective. It is not the coordinate centroid, and not the minimax 1-center, which minimizes the largest distance. If stage 10 split an HDBSCAN* cluster, SIGMA computes this 1-median separately for every final child over all road vertices of its component, not only at its Gap medoid.

11Stage 11 · recovery

If the 1-median fails, the output says so

Primary method: compute demand-to-node road distances in batches and take the node with the smallest total.

Recovery path, used only if the primary method fails and continuation is enabled:

  1. Compute the arithmetic centroid of the cluster's projected coordinates.
  2. Find the nearest road edge within the cluster's road component.
  3. Snap the centroid to that edge.
  4. Select the nearer endpoint node; break exact ties by the smallest node ID.

The field center_method separates network_1_median from euclidean_centroid_road_snap_fallback.

12Stage 12

Network Voronoi: which cluster owns each road?

For each economic type separately, all cluster-member road nodes are sources, not only the centers. Multi-source Dijkstra gives every reachable road node to its nearest source's cluster. Exact ties go to the smaller cluster label.

The search spreads from all sources at once. Where two searches meet, the road changes owner.

12Stage 12 · inside an edge

Where exactly does an edge change owner?

Take an edge (u, v) of length L. Du and Dv are the distances from the owners' nearest sources to u and v. A point at offset t from u costs Du + t via A, and Dv + L − t via B. They are equal at

t* = Dv + L − Du2   (clipped to [0, L])
100 m 300 m

The road is split by road distance, not at the middle of the edge.

12Stage 12 · 2-D rendering

Turning road ownership into areas

Graph X needs areas, because it tests whether territories overlap. SIGMA renders the 1-D ownership as 2-D polygons:

  1. Overlay a regular grid on the exact study boundary.
  2. Attach each cell's representative point to the nearest source-reachable road edge.
  3. Read the exact network-Voronoi owner at that point.
  4. Dissolve the cells by (type, cluster).

Within one type, each cell has exactly one owner, so the polygons of one type cover the area without overlap.

How fine must the grid be? A cell takes one owner, so a cell crossed by a boundary still has only one owner, and a very small territory can receive no cell at all. The run starts with 50 m cells. Each time a grid works, SIGMA halves the cell size, renders again and measures the changed-owner area: the share of the study area whose owning cluster differs between the two grids. It stops when that share is at most 0.5 % and keeps the finer grid. If a cluster receives no cell, the size is halved without a comparison.

Limits: at most one million candidate cells over the bounding box of the boundary (a start grid above that is made coarser, toward the finest size that fits by 12 bisections) and at most five halvings. When a limit stops refinement, SIGMA keeps the finest grid that worked. In a bounding box larger than about 625 km², even 25 m cells do not fit, so the first surface is kept without a comparison. The measure is a share of the whole area, so a small territory can still change while the sector counts as converged. partition_diagnostics.json records, for each sector, the kept cell size and why refinement stopped.

The exact object is road ownership. The polygon is a finite-resolution rendering of it, not an exact planar boundary, and most of its area contains no establishment. Being inside a polygon does not by itself give an unclustered establishment cluster membership or a SIGMA score: Explorer may display such places inside highlighted polygons of their own economic sector only as opt-in geographic context. Convergence means the rendering no longer depends much on the cell size; it does not make the boundary exact. If no grid gives a network surface, the labeled fallback is a Euclidean Voronoi of the cluster centers.

Part 3 · Stage 13 · sigma run

From transactions to a directed economic network

An input–output table says how much each sector buys from each other sector. SIGMA uses the table aggregated to the area's effective sectors (stages 5 and 9) and turns it into directed edges between sectors.

13Stage 13

A table of intermediate use

In the transaction matrix Z = (zij), row i is the supplying sector and column j is the using sector. Values must be finite and non-negative.

Example: zFood,Retail = 40 means the retail sector buys 40 units of food-sector output as an intermediate input.

The table summarizes flows between whole sectors of one economy. It does not identify which establishments trade with each other.

13Stage 13 · coefficients

Divide by the user's total output

aij = zijxj

xj is the total output of using sector j. It includes value added, so it is larger than the column sum of Z (30 and 45 here).

aFood,Retail = 40/200 = 0.20: retail uses 0.20 units of food-sector input per unit of retail output.

Every aij > 0 gives a directed edge i → j. The direction “supplier → user” stays the same through graph X.

13Stage 13 · default graph

The economy is not forced into a DAG

By default, transaction_preprocessing = none: the effective table is the raw transaction table (unless a valid explicit coefficient matrix is supplied under the compatibility rules).

Positive self-use (diagonal), two-way edges, and directed cycles are all kept. A cyclic economy is normal; treating cycles as errors would remove real interindustry dependence.

13Optional branch

Greedy MWAS, then RAS (not the default)

mwas_ras_fast exists for large or special tables when a sparse support is useful.

  1. Turn each positive off-diagonal zij into a candidate edge i → j; sort from largest to smallest.
  2. Accept u → v only if the accepted graph has no path v ⇝ u.
  3. Restore positive diagonal cells. Test with a bipartite max-flow problem whether the kept cells can match the original row and column totals; if not, restore removed cells in descending weight until they can.
  4. Balance with RAS (alternating row and column scaling) to the original margins; tolerances 10−8 absolute, 10−10 relative, at most 10,000 iterations.

This is a greedy approximation to a maximum-weight acyclic subgraph, not an exact optimum. Restored cells can close cycles again, so the final support need not be acyclic. Coefficients are recomputed from the balanced table; MWAS weights are never used downstream.

Part 4 · Stages 14–17 · sigma run

Constructing and scoring the spatial–economic graph

Clusters become nodes. Economic coefficients and territory overlap decide the edges. Katz centrality measures each node, and each establishment receives its cluster's score.

14Stage 14

Build X: overlap decides which edges can exist

Every retained cluster becomes a node u = (t, c). Isolated nodes are kept. A candidate edge u → v needs both conditions:

[at(u),t(v) > 0] ∧ [area(Pu ∩ Pv) > 0]

The first is economic (supplier type → user type). The second is spatial: the two cluster polygons overlap with strictly positive area. Overlap decides whether an edge exists; the overlap area is not part of the weight.

Here F2 and R1 do not overlap, so there is no edge between them.

14Stage 14 · weights

Economic coupling combines sector shares and road distance

Cluster shares count clustered establishments per exact economic type; the worked example uses 1/2 per cluster. Unclustered places are excluded from the totals.

wuv = ast · (Nu/Ns) · (Nv/Nt) / max(1, (ru + dR(mu, mv) + rv) / 1 m)λX

mu and mv are the two cluster centers. An edge is kept only if the centers are connected by road and the weight is strictly positive.

For a fixed coefficient, more separation gives less weight. With λX = 1, a 500 m route keeps 1/500 of the coefficient; a 1,400 m route keeps 1/1,400. This schematic sets the within-cluster mean road radii to zero; in a real run both radii are added to the center route before attenuation. In this schematic, two equally sized clusters per sector give both sector shares 1/2, making every edge 1/4 of its distance-only weight. The one-metre floor caps the distance factor at 1. The worked example highlights the strongest attenuated link, which is not the longest one.

Same-cluster self-edges remain omitted by an explicit topology rule, even though capped decay at 0 m equals 1. Disconnected centers produce a diagnostic, not an edge. Two-way coefficients still produce two-way edges when territories overlap. The X exponent is separate from point-score tempering.

15Stage 15

Why count paths of many steps?

An edge is a direct relation. A cluster can also reach others in 2, 3, or more steps. Katz centrality adds up walks (nodes may repeat) and multiplies each extra step by the attenuation α:

(I − αW)−1 = I + αW + α2W2 + …

(Wk)ij adds, over all k-step walks from i to j, the product of their edge weights. The walk in the figure adds α2 × (0.20×0.25/500) × (0.10×0.25/1,400) to the outgoing score of F2 and to the incoming score of F1.

15Stage 15 · directed Katz

A receiving score and a sending score

Kin = β(I − αWT)−11,   Kout = β(I − αW)−11

Kin adds the weighted walks that end at a node; Kout adds those that start there. Both use β = 1 and the same α:

α = 0.85 / ρ(W)

ρ(W) is the spectral radius (largest absolute eigenvalue). This keeps αρ(W) < 1, so the series converges. If X is a DAG, ρ(W) = 0 and SIGMA uses the largest row sum as the scale instead.

0.85

Because α is set relative to ρ(W), multiplying every weight by the same constant (for example metres to kilometres) does not change K. An isolated node keeps Kin = Kout = 1.

16Stage 16

Combine the two roles: the geometric mean

gi = √(Kini Kouti),   Ci = gi / ‖g‖2

Both roles matter, and a weak side limits the result. The geometric mean ranks nodes in the same order as the product KinKout (Sharkey, 2017), on the scale of a single Katz score. SIGMA normalizes once, at the end.

5.0

For nodes A (9, 1), B (5, 5), and C (1, 9): g = 3, 5, 3 and C = 0.457, 0.762, 0.457. The rule “geometric mean, then one final L2 normalization” is a SIGMA construction motivated by the published sender–receiver Katz product; it is not a separately published named index.

17Stage 17

Transfer cluster centrality to establishments

For establishment p in cluster c, dp is its road distance to the cluster's 1-median, and Q.90,c is the 90th percentile of these distances in the cluster.

qp = min(dp / max(Q.90,c, 1 m), 1),   Sp = Cc(1 − λqp),   λ = 0.15
200 m

So Cc(1 − λ) ≤ Sp ≤ Cc: position changes the score by at most 15%. An establishment at the edge of a cluster with Cc = 0.40 (0.34) still scores above one at the center of a cluster with Cc = 0.30.

The p90 gives each cluster its own distance scale; the cap at 1 stops very distant points from receiving an unbounded penalty; the 1 m floor avoids division by zero. This step does not estimate a separate place-to-place trade network.

✓All stages

One establishment, from record to score

  1. 1–2 One OSM record and one Overture record pass the distance and name rule → one canonical bakery.
  2. 3–4 Categories → a PSIC bakery code → candidate IO industries; no ownership question arises for a bakery.
  3. 5 Enough bakeries carry the same exact IO240 tag, so the area keeps it as its own sector (X:Y:Z).
  4. 6–8 The bakery snaps to a road and joins a baseline HDBSCAN* cluster of its sector.
  5. 9 Its sector has K ≥ 1, so the roll-up does not move it.
  6. 10 The one Gap pass splits the dispersed cluster in two; the bakery belongs to one final child.
  7. 11–12 That child's center is its own network 1-median; network Voronoi gives its territory.
  8. 13–14 aFood,Retail > 0, the territories overlap, and the centers are road-connected → a directed X edge.
  9. 15–16 Balanced Katz gives the cluster Cc = 0.40.
  10. 17 The bakery is 200 m from its center and Q.90,c = 800 m → Sp = 0.40 × (1 − 0.15 × 0.25) = 0.385.

Each final number has a traceable chain: matched identity → activity and IO evidence → area sector → roads → baseline cluster → final cluster → graph → score.

Part 5 · Publication · sigma run export and sigma view

Routes, auditability, adjustments, and limits

How published results reach the browser, what a result can and cannot support, how SIGMA reuses and recomputes its stages, and what to inspect before you interpret a score. Publication changes no number computed in stages 1–17.

→Publication · numbers

Graph X stores distances, not route lines

Stage 14 needs only the shortest road distance between two centers. It never stores the route line. Each published edge in sigma_X_edges.csv carries the coefficient, road_distance (center to center), effective_road_distance (plus both mean radii) and the weight.

A route line is needed only when a person looks at an edge on the map. By default (--route-geometry browser) SIGMA publishes a compact road graph and lets the browser draw routes on request. --route-geometry precomputed instead writes every route into the GeoPackage paths layer at export.

Both modes publish the same numerical X, the same centrality and the same scores.

→Publication · routing graph

Publish a compact road graph without establishment positions

During analysis, every establishment's snap point is inserted into the road graph as an extra vertex. Before publishing, SIGMA removes these vertices again: inside each original straight road segment, consecutive pieces are joined and their lengths are added. Original road vertices and the cluster-center vertices are kept.

Shortest-path distances between kept vertices do not change, because the pieces lie on one straight segment. The file sigma_routing_graph.bin holds only coordinates of kept vertices and edges with lengths (format SIGRTE01).

If a segment is not a simple straight chain, export stops instead of publishing an unsafe graph. The metadata records routing_graph_privacy = base_vertices_and_x_centers_only.

→Publication · route on request

Draw a route on request and check its length

When you select an edge in Explorer, a Web Worker (a background browser thread) runs Dijkstra's algorithm on the compact graph from the source center to the target center. Before drawing, it compares the route length with the published road_distance:

|dbrowser − dpublished| ≤ max(10−6, 10−8 · dpublished)

If the lengths disagree, no route is drawn and an error is shown. Up to 256 recent routes are cached.

When two routes have exactly the same length, the browser may draw the other one. The enforced contract is equal length, not identical coordinates.

Supported reading

  • Spatial concentration under a specified road metric
  • Modeled IO-compatible links between overlapping territories
  • Balanced, directed, walk-based network centrality
  • Transparent, reproducible comparison of scores within one run

Unsupported reading

  • Verified purchases or supply contracts between firms
  • Travel time, legal one-way driving, or traffic congestion
  • Precise planar territorial borders
  • Causal economic influence or policy impact

Reproducibility: an artifact dependency DAG

Each managed stage has a recipe: parameters, schema and implementation versions, upstream artifact identities, and source hashes. The recipe is stored as canonical JSON and hashed with SHA-256; the output files are hashed too. When a parameter changes, only the stages that depend on it are recomputed. Choose a change:

Long stages (clustering, partitions, X, export) also keep savepoints: completed work units per sector, checked by SHA-256, so an interrupted run resumes instead of starting again. Savepoints are cleared once the stage artifact is committed. Reproducible computation does not by itself validate the modeling assumptions.

Inspect before you interpret: identity and geography

  • Cross-source matches, augmentation sources and unresolved classes
  • Administrative-office changes, institution_promotion_status and economy_review_required
  • Stage 5 sectors, economy_status and coarse-place policy
  • Boundary, road, and snap quality
  • HDBSCAN* noise, network fallbacks and adaptive-radius diagnostics
  • rollup_audit.json: tested and accepted merges; original_economy_code versus economy_code
  • median_gap_audit.json: selected_k, outcome, child IDs, sampled-fit flag
  • center_method and surface_method fallbacks

Inspect before you interpret: economy and scores

  • Raw and effective transaction matrices, and coefficient direction
  • Overlap qualification and unreachable pairs
  • aij, road_distance, effective_road_distance, sector shares, wuv
  • katz_in_raw, katz_out_raw, final C and Sp

Priority sensitivity tests. Before making substantive claims, compare matching and classification thresholds, road profiles, HDBSCAN* horizons, Voronoi cell sizes, IO definitions, and alternatives to the distance-multiplying X weight.

Notation

  • GR, dR: undirected road graph and road shortest-path distance
  • (t, c): sector (economic type) and cluster → one X node
  • X:Y:Z, X:Y:o, X:Y, X:o, X: sparse sector codes; M: sector membership matrix
  • K: number of retained clusters of one sector (stage 9)
  • core(p), dmr, λ = 1/dmr: HDBSCAN* quantities
  • Wk, Gap(k), sk, B: k-medoid cost, Gap statistic, standard error, reference draws (stage 10)
  • Z, xj, aij = zij/xj: transactions, output, coefficients
  • W = (wuv): weighted adjacency of X
  • Kin, Kout, C: raw Katz scores and balanced centrality
  • Sp, Q.90,c, λ (scoring): point score, p90 distance, tempering

The symbol λ has three unrelated meanings: the HDBSCAN* density level, the X distance exponent λX, and the scoring tempering factor. W without subscript is the adjacency of X; Wk is the stage 10 cost.
